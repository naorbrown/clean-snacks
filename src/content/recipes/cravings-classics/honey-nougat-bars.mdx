## Steps

1. Toast the almonds on a baking sheet at 350°F (175°C) for 8 to 10 minutes until fragrant and lightly golden. Set aside. Line an 8x8-inch baking pan with parchment paper, leaving overhang on two sides.

2. Heat the honey in a small heavy-bottomed saucepan over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until it reaches 260°F (127°C) on a candy thermometer. While the honey heats, whip the egg whites and sea salt with a stand mixer or hand mixer until stiff peaks form.

3. With the mixer running on medium speed, pour the hot honey in a slow, thin stream into the whipped egg whites. Continue beating for 6 to 8 minutes until the nougat is very thick, glossy, and pulls away from the sides of the bowl. Beat in the vanilla extract. Quickly fold in the toasted almonds with a spatula.

4. Scrape the nougat into the prepared pan and press it flat with an oiled spatula or oiled hands. The nougat is very sticky, so oiling your tools is essential. Let it cool to room temperature, then refrigerate for 20 minutes until firm. Lift the slab out, and cut into 12 bars.

5. Melt the dark chocolate with the coconut oil using a double boiler or microwave. Dip each bar in the chocolate, coating all sides. Place on parchment paper and refrigerate for 10 minutes until the chocolate is set.

## Why It Works

Traditional triangular nougat candy bars use sugar, glucose syrup, and milk chocolate with added vegetable fats. This version builds chewy nougat from just honey and egg whites, the way nougat was originally made centuries ago. Honey provides natural sweetness along with trace minerals and enzymes. The whipped egg whites create the signature airy, chewy texture that stretches when you bite into it. Whole toasted almonds add crunch, protein, and vitamin E, while the dark chocolate coating delivers rich cacao flavor without refined sugar.

## Tips

- **Temperature precision.** A candy thermometer is non-negotiable for nougat. Even a few degrees off will change the texture from chewy to rock-hard or too soft.
- **Oil everything.** Nougat sticks to everything. Oil your spatula, hands, and knife before handling it.
- **Triangular shape.** For a triangular profile, pour the nougat into a parchment-lined triangular mold or shape it by hand once slightly cooled.
- **Honey flavor matters.** Use a mild honey like clover or acacia. Strong honeys like buckwheat will overpower the chocolate.
