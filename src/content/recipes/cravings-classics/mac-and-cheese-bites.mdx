## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 375°F (190°C). Grease a 24-cup mini muffin tin generously with butter to prevent sticking.

2. Cook the elbow macaroni one minute short of al dente — it will continue cooking in the oven. Drain and return to the pot off the heat.

3. Stir the melted butter, milk, beaten eggs, sea salt, black pepper, and smoked paprika into the cooked pasta. Add 1 1/2 cups of the shredded cheddar and fold until every piece of pasta is coated in a thick, cheesy mixture.

4. Spoon the mac and cheese into the prepared muffin cups, pressing down gently to compact each one. Top each bite with a pinch of the remaining cheddar.

5. Bake for 18-20 minutes until the edges are golden and crispy and the tops are set. Let them cool in the tin for 5 minutes before using a butter knife to gently pop each bite out.

## Why It Works

The muffin tin is the key here — it creates a high ratio of crispy, golden edges to creamy interior in every single bite. The egg acts as a binder that holds the mac together so you can pick them up without the whole thing falling apart, while the cheese melts into a cohesive sauce without needing any flour-based roux or processed cheese product. Sharp cheddar provides enough flavor intensity that you don't need a laundry list of additives to make it taste rich. These satisfy the comfort food craving because they deliver the exact same gooey-meets-crunchy contrast as the best baked mac and cheese, just in a grab-and-go format.

## Tips

- **Undercook the pasta slightly.** It bakes for another 20 minutes in the oven. If you cook it fully before baking, the noodles turn mushy and lose their structure inside the bites.
- **Let them cool before removing.** The egg needs time to set as it cools. If you try to pop them out immediately they will crumble. Five minutes of patience is the difference between clean bites and a mess.
- **Use freshly shredded cheese.** Pre-shredded cheese contains anti-caking starches that prevent it from melting smoothly. Shred a block yourself for the best creamy texture.
- **Reheat in the oven, not the microwave.** A few minutes at 350°F restores the crispy edges. The microwave makes them soggy.
