## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 400°F (200°C). Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.

2. Drain and rinse the chickpeas thoroughly. Spread them on a clean kitchen towel and roll them around to dry completely. Remove any loose skins that come off — they burn in the oven and create an acrid taste.

3. Toss the dried chickpeas with avocado oil, smoked paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, cumin, sea salt, and black pepper. Spread them in a single layer on the baking sheet.

4. Bake for 25-30 minutes, shaking the pan once halfway through. They're done when they're deeply golden, shrunken slightly, and make a rattling sound when you shake the pan. They will continue to crisp as they cool.

5. Let them cool completely on the pan before eating or storing. They must be fully cooled to reach maximum crunch.

## Why It Works

The key to the crunch is moisture removal. Chickpeas are about 60% water, and roasting at high heat drives that moisture out, leaving behind a puffed, crunchy shell around a slightly creamy interior. Drying them thoroughly before roasting is essential because surface moisture creates steam that prevents the exterior from crisping. The seasoning sticks best when applied before baking — the oil acts as glue while the heat toasts the spices directly onto the surface. Chickpeas deliver about 7 grams of protein and 6 grams of fiber per serving, which means your brain gets the hand-to-mouth crunching satisfaction it's looking for while your body actually gets something useful. That's the difference between stress eating that leaves you feeling worse and stress eating that doesn't.

## Tips

- **Dry them obsessively.** This is the single most important step. Wet chickpeas will never get crunchy no matter how long you roast them. Spend a full minute rolling them in the towel.
- **Remove the loose skins.** The papery skins that slide off during drying will burn and smoke in the oven. Pick them out before roasting.
- **Don't underbake.** If they're still soft when you pull them out, they'll turn chewy once they cool. Push past golden to deeply golden — they should feel hard and light when done.
- **Store them right.** An airtight container at room temperature keeps them crunchy for days. Any moisture — like a refrigerator — will soften them within hours.
