## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 325 degrees F. Grease 8 wells of a canoe-shaped snack cake pan or a mini loaf pan with coconut oil. In a large bowl, beat the eggs with an electric mixer on high speed for 3 minutes until thick and pale yellow.

2. Drizzle in the honey and vanilla while continuing to beat on medium speed. In a separate bowl, whisk together the coconut flour, tapioca starch, and baking powder. Gently fold the dry mixture into the egg batter in two additions, being careful not to deflate the eggs.

3. Divide the batter evenly among the prepared wells, filling each about two-thirds full. Bake for 18 to 22 minutes until the tops spring back when lightly pressed and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. Let cool in the pan for 5 minutes, then turn out onto a wire rack to cool completely.

4. While the cakes cool, scoop the solid cream from the top of the chilled coconut cream can into a bowl, discarding the liquid. Add the maple syrup and whip with an electric mixer on high for 2 minutes until fluffy peaks form.

5. Using a long thin tip or a chopstick, poke a hole into the bottom of each cooled cake and wiggle it to create a small cavity. Transfer the whipped coconut cream to a piping bag fitted with a small round tip and pipe the cream into each cake until it feels heavy and full.

6. Serve immediately or refrigerate until ready to eat. For a classic finish, dust the tops lightly with tapioca starch.

## Why It Works

The egg-heavy batter creates a tender, springy sponge that mimics the airy texture of commercial snack cakes without relying on bleached flour or chemical emulsifiers. Coconut flour absorbs moisture and gives the crumb a soft, fine texture, while tapioca starch adds lightness. The whipped coconut cream filling delivers the same rich mouthfeel as hydrogenated vegetable shortening filling but with beneficial medium-chain fatty acids instead of trans fats. Unlike store-bought versions that list over 30 ingredients including polysorbate 60 and cellulose gum, this recipe uses eight whole-food ingredients.

## Tips

- **Chill the coconut cream overnight.** This is essential. The fat must solidify completely so it whips into stable peaks rather than staying liquid.
- **Room temperature eggs matter.** Cold eggs do not whip to full volume. Set them on the counter 30 minutes before starting or place them in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Storage trick.** Freeze unfilled cakes in a single layer, then bag them. Fill with fresh cream on the day you plan to eat them for the best texture.
- **No canoe pan.** Use a standard muffin tin instead. The shape will differ but the flavor and filling technique work the same way.
