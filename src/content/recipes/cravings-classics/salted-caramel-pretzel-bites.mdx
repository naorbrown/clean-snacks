## Steps

1. Stir the yeast and honey into the warm water. Let it sit for 5 minutes until foamy. Add the flour, sea salt, and melted butter. Mix until a shaggy dough forms, then knead on a floured surface for 4-5 minutes until smooth and elastic. Cover and rest for 10 minutes.

2. While the dough rests, make the date caramel. Soak the pitted dates in hot water for 5 minutes, then drain. Blend the dates with coconut cream, vanilla extract, and a pinch of sea salt until completely smooth and glossy. Set aside.

3. Preheat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Line a baking sheet with parchment paper. Bring a large pot of water to a boil and add the baking soda.

4. Divide the dough into 4 pieces. Roll each piece into a long rope about 1/2-inch thick, then cut into 1-inch nuggets. Working in batches, drop the nuggets into the boiling baking soda water for 30 seconds. Remove with a slotted spoon and place on the prepared baking sheet.

5. Brush each boiled pretzel bite with melted butter and sprinkle generously with flaky sea salt. Bake for 12-14 minutes until deep golden brown.

6. Serve the warm pretzel bites alongside the date caramel for dipping. The caramel can also be drizzled directly over the top.

## Why It Works

The baking soda bath is the entire secret to pretzel flavor and texture. It creates an alkaline environment on the surface of the dough that triggers the Maillard reaction at oven temperatures, producing that signature dark, chewy pretzel crust you cannot get any other way. The date caramel mimics traditional caramel's thick, sticky sweetness because dates are naturally high in glucose and fructose, and blending them with coconut cream emulsifies the sugars into a smooth, pourable sauce. The sweet-salty combination works because salt amplifies sweetness while suppressing bitterness — your brain registers the flavors as more intense and more satisfying than either one alone.

## Tips

- **Don't skip the baking soda bath.** It is non-negotiable. Without it, you get bread nuggets, not pretzel bites. The 30-second dip creates the chewy, dark exterior.
- **Use really hot water for soaking dates.** Nearly boiling water softens them enough to blend completely smooth. Lukewarm water leaves fibrous chunks in your caramel.
- **Bake until deeply golden.** Pale pretzels taste doughy. Push the color further than you think — pretzel bites should be a rich mahogany brown.
- **Eat them warm.** Pretzels are at their best fresh from the oven. They stiffen as they cool and lose that pillowy interior.
