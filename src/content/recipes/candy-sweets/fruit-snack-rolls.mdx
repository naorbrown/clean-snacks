## Steps

1. Preheat your oven to 170 degrees Fahrenheit (or the lowest setting). Line a large rimmed baking sheet with a silicone baking mat or parchment paper. Do not grease the surface.

2. Blend the strawberries, applesauce, and raw honey in a blender or food processor until completely smooth. Taste and add more honey if the berries are tart. The puree should be pourable but not watery.

3. Pour the puree onto the prepared baking sheet and spread it into a thin, even layer about 1/8-inch thick using an offset spatula. Take your time here — uneven spots will dry too quickly and crack while thicker areas stay sticky.

4. Place the baking sheet in the oven and prop the door open slightly with a wooden spoon to allow moisture to escape. Dry for 3 to 4 hours, checking every hour. The fruit leather is done when it feels dry and slightly tacky to the touch but peels away from the mat cleanly without sticking.

5. Remove from the oven and let it cool completely on the baking sheet. Peel the fruit leather off the mat in one large sheet, then cut into strips with kitchen scissors or a pizza cutter.

6. Roll each strip in a piece of parchment paper for storage and easy snacking. Bundle the rolls together in a zip-top bag or airtight container.

## Why It Works

Drying fruit at low temperature concentrates the natural sugars and flavors while preserving more vitamins and enzymes than high-heat processing. Applesauce provides pectin, a natural fiber that gives the leather its smooth, bendable texture. Using whole fruit instead of fruit concentrate means you keep all the fiber, which slows sugar absorption and keeps energy steady.

## Tips

- **Even spreading is everything.** Use an offset spatula and spend an extra minute getting the layer truly uniform. This single step determines whether you get pliable rolls or a mix of crispy edges and gooey centers.
- **Flavor variations.** Swap strawberries for mango, peach, or mixed berries. Adding a squeeze of lemon juice to any fruit brightens the flavor after drying.
- **Dehydrator option.** If you have a food dehydrator, spread the puree on dehydrator sheets at 135 degrees Fahrenheit for 6-8 hours. The result is more consistent than oven drying.
