## Steps

1. Pour the whole milk into a small saucepan over medium-low heat. Warm it slowly, stirring occasionally, until you see the first wisps of steam rising from the surface. Do not let it boil — overheated milk develops a scorched flavor and forms a skin on top that nobody wants in their hot chocolate.

2. Add the raw cacao powder and whisk vigorously for 30-45 seconds until the powder is fully dissolved with no clumps remaining. A small whisk works best here. The cacao will resist mixing at first — keep whisking and it will incorporate smoothly into the warm milk.

3. Remove the saucepan from heat. Stir in the raw honey and vanilla extract until the honey is fully dissolved. Adding the honey off-heat preserves its beneficial enzymes. Add the tiny pinch of sea salt and stir once more.

4. Pour into mugs and serve immediately. For an extra touch, top with a dollop of fresh whipped cream made from heavy cream and a light dusting of cacao powder. Drink slowly — this is rich enough to savor.

## Why It Works

Instant hot cocoa packets are built on sugar — it's typically the first ingredient, followed by corn syrup solids, hydrogenated oils, and artificial flavors. Actual chocolate is often the last ingredient in trace amounts. This recipe flips that ratio entirely. Raw cacao powder is one of the most mineral-dense foods available, containing significant amounts of magnesium, iron, copper, and manganese per tablespoon. Magnesium alone supports over 300 enzymatic reactions in the body, including muscle relaxation and nervous system function — which is one reason why chocolate feels so comforting. Whole milk provides the fat needed to carry fat-soluble vitamins A, D, and K2, and creates the creamy body that makes hot chocolate satisfying. Sea salt amplifies the chocolate flavor at a concentration too low to taste as salty, and adds trace minerals to an already nutrient-rich drink.

## Tips

- **Cacao vs. cocoa matters.** Raw cacao powder is made from cold-pressed, unroasted cacao beans and retains more antioxidants and minerals than Dutch-processed cocoa, which is treated with alkali. Look for "raw cacao" on the label. The flavor is slightly more complex and less sweet than standard cocoa.
- **Frothy finish.** Before pouring, blend the hot chocolate in a blender for 10 seconds or use a milk frother directly in the saucepan. This creates a velvety, cafe-style foam on top that elevates the entire experience.
- **Date syrup swap.** If you prefer a deeper caramel sweetness, replace the raw honey with an equal amount of date syrup. Dates are rich in potassium, fiber, and iron, and their molasses-like sweetness pairs beautifully with the bitterness of real cacao.
