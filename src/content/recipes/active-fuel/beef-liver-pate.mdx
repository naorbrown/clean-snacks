## Steps

1. Soak the beef liver chunks in a bowl of cold water with a squeeze of lemon juice for 30 minutes. This draws out residual blood and mellows the mineral flavor. Drain, pat completely dry with paper towels, and season with half the sea salt.

2. Melt 2 tablespoons of butter in a large cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat. Add the diced onion and cook for 6-8 minutes, stirring occasionally, until deeply golden and soft. Add the garlic and thyme, stir for 30 seconds until fragrant, then transfer everything to a food processor bowl.

3. Return the skillet to high heat and add 2 more tablespoons of butter. Once the butter foams and the foam subsides, lay the liver pieces in a single layer. Sear for 2-3 minutes per side until browned on the outside but still slightly pink in the center. Overcooking liver makes the pate grainy and bitter.

4. Transfer the seared liver to the food processor with the onion mixture. Add the remaining 2 tablespoons of butter, the rest of the sea salt, and the black pepper. Blend on high for 60-90 seconds, scraping down the sides once, until completely smooth and creamy.

5. Taste and adjust seasoning. Spoon the pate into a jar or ramekin, press plastic wrap directly onto the surface to prevent oxidation, and refrigerate for at least 2 hours to firm up before serving.

## Why It Works

Beef liver is the single most nutrient-dense food available, delivering concentrated amounts of zinc, B12, retinol (true vitamin A), and heme iron in every serving. Butter provides fat-soluble vitamins A and K2 while giving the pate its smooth, spreadable texture. Slow-cooking the onions before blending adds a natural sweetness that balances the liver's mineral intensity.

## Tips

- **Texture.** For an ultra-smooth pate, push the blended mixture through a fine-mesh sieve. This removes any remaining connective tissue and produces a restaurant-quality result.
- **Serving.** Spread on sourdough toast, seed crackers, or cucumber rounds. A small serving — about two tablespoons — delivers a full day's worth of B12.
- **Sourcing.** Always choose grass-fed liver. The nutrient profile is significantly higher than grain-fed, particularly in fat-soluble vitamins and omega-3 fatty acids.
