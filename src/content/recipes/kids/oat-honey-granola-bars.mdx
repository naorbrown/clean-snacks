## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 325°F (160°C). Line an 8x8 inch baking pan with parchment paper, leaving overhang on two sides for easy removal.

2. Spread the rolled oats on a baking sheet in an even layer. Toast in the oven for 8-10 minutes, stirring once halfway through, until they smell nutty and turn lightly golden. Toasting develops a deeper flavor and removes excess moisture, which helps the bars stay crisp instead of turning chewy and damp.

3. While the oats toast, warm the honey, peanut butter, and coconut oil together in a small saucepan over low heat. Stir until everything melts together into a smooth, pourable mixture. Remove from heat and stir in the vanilla extract and sea salt.

4. Pour the warm honey mixture over the toasted oats in a large bowl. Stir thoroughly until every oat is coated. Let the mixture cool for 5 minutes, then fold in the chocolate chips if using — adding them to the hot mixture will cause them to melt.

5. Transfer the mixture into the prepared baking pan. Press it down firmly and evenly using the back of a measuring cup or your hands with a piece of parchment on top. This is the most important step — bars that aren't pressed firmly enough will crumble apart. Push especially hard into the corners and edges.

6. Bake for 18-22 minutes until the edges are golden brown. The center will still look soft, but it firms up as it cools. Let the bars cool completely in the pan — at least 30 minutes — before lifting them out by the parchment overhang and cutting into 12 bars.

## Why It Works

Commercial granola bars rely on corn syrup, soybean oil, sugar, and a web of binding agents to hold their shape and extend shelf life. The result is a product that spikes blood sugar as fast as candy despite its "healthy" branding. This version uses three binding ingredients that actually contribute nutrition: raw honey provides glucose and fructose along with trace minerals, enzymes, and antimicrobial compounds; peanut butter delivers protein, magnesium, and healthy fats that slow sugar absorption; coconut oil contains medium-chain triglycerides that the body uses for quick energy rather than storing as fat. Rolled oats form the base, contributing soluble fiber that feeds beneficial gut bacteria and helps stabilize blood sugar. Together, these ingredients create a bar that holds together, tastes great, and provides sustained energy instead of a spike and crash.

## Tips

- **Press hard.** Seriously. The number one reason homemade granola bars crumble is insufficient pressing. Use real force when compacting the mixture into the pan. You want it dense and tight.
- **Cool completely before cutting.** Cutting warm bars produces ragged, crumbly pieces. Full cooling allows the honey and coconut oil to firm up, creating a clean cut. If you're in a hurry, refrigerate for 15 minutes.
- **Add-ins to try.** Swap the chocolate chips for dried cranberries, pumpkin seeds, sunflower seeds, or shredded coconut. Keep the total add-in volume to about 1/4-1/3 cup so the bar ratio stays balanced.
