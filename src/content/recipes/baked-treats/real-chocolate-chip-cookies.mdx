## Steps

1. Brown the butter by melting it in a light-colored saucepan over medium heat, swirling occasionally, until the milk solids turn golden and it smells nutty, about 4-5 minutes. Pour the browned butter into a large mixing bowl and let it cool for 5 minutes.

2. Add the coconut sugar to the warm browned butter and whisk until combined. Beat in the egg, egg yolk, and vanilla extract until the mixture is smooth and slightly thickened, about 1 minute of vigorous whisking.

3. In a separate bowl, whisk together the oat flour, baking soda, and sea salt. Fold the dry ingredients into the wet ingredients until just combined — do not overmix. Stir in the dark chocolate chips. For best results, cover and refrigerate the dough for at least 30 minutes (or up to 48 hours).

4. Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C) and line baking sheets with parchment paper. Scoop rounded tablespoons of dough onto the sheets about 2 inches apart. Sprinkle each cookie with a few flakes of sea salt. Bake for 9-11 minutes until the edges are golden but the centers still look slightly underdone. Let them cool on the baking sheet for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack.

## Why It Works

Browning the butter transforms ordinary cookies into something extraordinary — the Maillard reaction creates hundreds of new flavor compounds that deliver deep caramel and toffee notes impossible to achieve with plain melted butter. Coconut sugar dissolves beautifully into the warm fat and brings a natural caramel sweetness along with trace minerals like potassium, zinc, and iron, unlike refined white sugar which is stripped of all nutrients. The extra egg yolk adds richness and creates that coveted fudgy center. Dark chocolate chips with 85%+ cacao are loaded with magnesium, iron, and antioxidant flavanols, making them a genuinely nutritious swap for the waxy, low-cacao chips in commercial cookies. Sea salt on top amplifies sweetness while delivering essential trace minerals.

## Tips

- **Chill the dough.** Resting the dough in the fridge for at least 30 minutes (ideally overnight) lets the oat flour fully hydrate, concentrates flavors, and produces thicker cookies with better texture. This is the single biggest upgrade you can make.
- **Use a cookie scoop.** A #40 (tablespoon-sized) cookie scoop ensures every cookie bakes evenly. Press a few extra chocolate chips into the tops of each ball before baking for that bakery look.
- **Store smart.** Keep cooled cookies in an airtight container at room temperature. To restore just-baked texture, warm them in a 300°F oven for 3-4 minutes. The dough balls also freeze perfectly — bake from frozen, adding 1-2 extra minutes.
